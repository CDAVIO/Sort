Features

Everything the camera already knew.

It reads the file, not the folder

Camera model, capture time and GPS come straight out of EXIF, HEIC and QuickTime metadata, not from whatever the file happens to be called.

You decide the shape

Device, year, month, day, place, in any order you like. A file missing one of them simply loses that level instead of landing in a junk folder.

Look before you commit

Preview builds the whole tree and writes nothing at all. Copy keeps your originals untouched. Move frees the space.

Place names without the internet

GPS coordinates are matched to a city from a list built into the app. No lookup leaves your machine, so nobody learns where your photos were taken.

Rename, or don't

Keep every filename byte for byte, or rebuild them from the date, the device, the city and a counter. The extension is never touched.

Built for big libraries

100 GB uses about as much memory as 100 MB, because files are streamed rather than loaded. Scanning reads only each file's header.

Questions

Five things worth knowing.

Do my photos get uploaded anywhere?

No. There is no server, no account and no analytics. SORT makes no network request at all once it is on your computer. Switch off the internet and it behaves exactly the same.

Is it really free?

Yes. No trial, no limits, no paid version, no advertising. It is a tool CDAV.IO built and gives away.

Can it overwrite or lose a photo?

No. Copy never touches your originals. If two files would land on the same name, the second becomes _2 and the third _3. Move copies first, checks the size matches, and only then removes the original. If a write fails, the original stays where it is.

What happens to a photo with no date or no GPS?

That level disappears from its path. A photo with no location still gets its device and date folders. Nothing is dumped in an unknown bin unless you ask for one.

How large a library can it handle?

100 GB is fine. Files are streamed rather than loaded into memory, so a huge library uses about as much RAM as a small one. Expect two to four minutes to scan 100 GB, then your disk's own speed.
